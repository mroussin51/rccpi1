Set sane RPO/RTO targets

Define how much data you can afford to lose (RPO) and how long you can be down (RTO). Start with 24h/4h for small sites and refine as business needs grow. Align backup frequency and architecture to meet these targets without overspending.

Steps

  1. Agree on RPO/RTO with stakeholders.
  2. Match backup schedules to RPO.
  3. Design HA or warm‑standby to meet RTO.
  4. Test restores and failovers regularly.

What belongs in a backup/restore runbook

List systems, data locations, credentials, exact commands, and validation steps. Include who approves a restore and how to communicate status. Store the runbook in two places and test twice a year to keep it current.

Steps

  1. Inventory critical systems and data paths.
  2. Document commands and success checks.
  3. Define roles, approvals, and communications.
  4. Test and update on a set cadence.

HA vs DR: what you actually need

High availability keeps services running through localized failures; disaster recovery brings them back after major outages. Start with multi‑AZ and backups; consider multi‑region only if downtime costs justify the complexity.

Steps

  1. Enable multi‑AZ where supported.
  2. Back up configs and data frequently.
  3. Consider multi‑region for critical apps only.
  4. Review costs vs downtime impact annually.
